# KBosmos — solar system simulation

A single-file web app (`kbosmos.html`) that shows the Sun, planets, Moon and Pluto at their real current positions and animates orbits and spins at true relative speeds.

## Stack
- Plain HTML/CSS/JS, no build step. Three.js r128 (UMD global `THREE`) from cdnjs, plus `OrbitControls` from jsDelivr (`three@0.128.0/examples/js/controls/OrbitControls.js`).
- Surface maps live in `textures/` (`<bodyId>.jpg`, plus `earth_clouds.jpg`, `earth_night.jpg`, `saturn_ring.png`). They're from Wikimedia Commons: Solar System Scope maps (CC BY 4.0, credited in the panel) and NASA's New Horizons Pluto mosaic (public domain). Earth and Moon are 3840 px; the rest are 2k. The page doesn't load the image files directly. It loads `textures/js/<name>.js` (each calls `orreryMap(name, dataURI)`), built from the images by `.claude/build-maps.ps1` (which also writes the 1024 px `textures/js/sm/` copies for touch devices). That way the maps also work when `kbosmos.html` is opened straight from disk (Chrome blocks WebGL from using `file://` images). Re-run the build script after changing an image. The procedural canvas textures are kept as the fallback until the maps load. Earth city lights are an emissive map faded on the lit side via `onBeforeCompile` (uses Lambert's `vLightFront`).

## Physics
- Planet positions: NASA JPL "Approximate Positions of the Planets", Table 1 Keplerian elements (valid 1800–2050). Sim time is clamped to that range.
- Moon: `moonGeo`, the main terms of Meeus ch. 47 (~10″, ~50 km), with general precession (`PREC`) taken off so it's in the J2000 frame like the planets. The eclipse views need this accuracy, since the Moon is only 0.5° wide. `moonNode` subtracts the same precession.
- Spin: IAU pole RA/Dec and prime-meridian W = W0 + Wd·days. Each `tilt` frame has +Y = pole and +X = the node at RA = pole RA + 90°, and `spin.rotation.y = W + mapLon0`, so the map centre (sphere local +X) lands where the IAU puts it. That's why the Moon's near side faces Earth and Earth's day side matches UTC. Negative Wd = spin opposite to the listed pole (Venus, Uranus). Pluto's IAU pole is its right-hand pole, so its Wd is positive, with `retro: true`. Pluto's map is centred on 180° (`mapLon0`).
- "Watch from above" (Moon box in the panel): top-down view of Earth and the Moon with a gold near-side marker (`nearMark`) to show tidal locking.
- Coordinates: ecliptic J2000 (x, y, z) maps to scene (x, z, −y), so scene +Y is ecliptic north.
- One shared sim clock drives everything, which keeps relative speeds exact.
- Galaxy view (Galaxy button): one click enters it via `setGalaxy`. That sets time to 1 month/s and follows the Sun, shows a compact card (drift-scale buttons, plus folded-away legend, schematic map and facts), and streams a dust field (`dust`, wrapped box shifted by the drift since entry). Exit restores the previous speed, pause and focus. It also has 3D "spiral path" trails. The Sun's galactocentric velocity is `GAL_V` (233 km/s circular + Schönrich 2010 solar motion), converted galactic → equatorial (`A_G` transpose) → ecliptic → scene. The trails are recomputed every frame from `heliocentric()` at past times minus the drift (`galK` scales the drift: 1 = true, 0.1, 0.01), so the Sun and planets themselves never move. The HUD distance readout also shows the galactic distance travelled since the page opened.
- Moons of other planets (`MOONS`, 20 major moons): JPL mean elements (ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/sats/elem, epoch J2000), each in its Laplace-plane frame (or the planet's equator), built like the body `tilt` frames. Uranus's moons use its right-hand pole, opposite the IAU north pole, so they orbit the way Uranus spins. The display distance in planet radii is `moonDist`: true below 3 radii (keeps Mimas outside the rings), compressed beyond that. Moon maps (`textures/<moon>.jpg`, from NASA/USGS/JPL/LPI maps on Wikimedia Commons) are made from the downloaded originals by `.claude/prep-moon-maps.ps1`, which crops poster margins, reprojects Ganymede from Mercator and fills unmapped areas. They load per system on first follow or close-up (`loadMoonMaps`). The Cassini maps are centred on 180° (`MAP_CENTER_180`), the rest on 0°. Uranus moon maps are flipped north-up. The moons are tidally locked, facing their planet, and the procedural textures remain as the fallback. Each moon is also a followable `byId` entry (`isMoon`, with a stand-in `root` at its world position). Following a planet frames its whole moon system.
- Comets (`COMETS`, Comets button → `#cometPop`): ʻOumuamua, 2I/Borisov, 3I/ATLAS, Halley, Hale–Bopp, Hyakutake, NEOWISE and Tsuchinshan–ATLAS. Elements `[e, q, i, node, ω, tp JD]` are JPL Horizons osculating elements (heliocentric ecliptic J2000) taken at each perihelion. SBDB's epochs can be decades off (Halley's 1968 epoch puts the 1986 perihelion a day out), so they're not used. Halley has one set per return, and `cometSet` picks the one with the nearest perihelion. `cometPV` solves Kepler with a universal variable (Stumpff functions plus Laguerre), so ellipses, near-parabolas (Tsuchinshan–ATLAS e = 1.00002) and hyperbolas all work. Checked against Horizons vectors: ≤1e-5 AU near perihelion, ≤0.4° decades away. Tails are additive sprite chains (`cometsUpdate`). The ion tail points straight anti-sunward, and the dust tail curves back against the velocity. Both grow inside 3.5 AU, scaled by each comet's `tail` factor. They're illustrative, not modelled. Paths are drawn within 60 AU (`cometPath`) under the Orbits menu's "Comets" box. Comets are `byId` entries (`isComet`) so they can be followed, with `renderCometDetail` and side-on framing in `focusCamPos`. They're hidden in What if… (not part of the N-body simulation).
- Shoemaker–Levy 9 (`sl9`, its own section after the comets): Jupiter-centred JPL Horizons states. `SL9_REF` is fragment G every 8 days from 1988, plus twice a day around the 7 Jul 1992 break-up. `SL9_FRAGS` holds the 20 pieces Horizons has (J, M and P1 broke up or faded): each piece's impact time and its own state 2.5 days before it. Horizons treats Jupiter as a point, so the impact times are where each path meets the 1-bar ellipsoid (a 71492 km, c 66854 km), found by bisecting a Hermite interpolation of 5-minute vectors. They match the published times minus the ~42 min Jupiter–Earth light time to ~2 min, at about 44°S. Between samples `kepProp` does a two-body universal-variable propagation around Jupiter (`MU_JUP`). Before its last days each piece follows G's path time-shifted by a gap that grows from 0 at the break-up to its real impact gap (within 1% of Horizons). In its final day it blends onto its own orbit. Display distances use the moons' `moonDist`. Impacts trigger `impactShow` (the What if… effects, so `impactDraw` also runs outside fiction mode) when time passes them without jumping. Dark spots (`sl9Scars`) are shells on Jupiter's spin group, placed using the spin angle at impact time, and they fade over ~45 days. The Comets panel has Break-up / Watch G hit / All 20 impacts jumps (camera via `impactCam`), and the detail panel shows the next impact and the Earth-received time.
- Eclipses: computed at load (not hard-coded) with Meeus, *Astronomical Algorithms* ch. 54, for every lunation 1800–2050 → `ECLIPSES` (sorted, UTC ms). Uses a coarse ΔT table. Matches NASA catalogue times to ~1 min. The "Eclipses" button opens prev/next solar & lunar bookmarks plus a nearby list; jumping pauses and follows Earth. No shadows are rendered, so `tintMoon()` reddens the Moon while it's in the umbra.
- "Lined up: why this kind of eclipse?" (Eclipses panel, `lineUp` → `renderLine`/`renderSide`): live, from geometry rather than the catalogue. Solar mode: the shadow axis through the Sun's and Moon's centres. The to-scale view is from the point on Earth nearest that axis, so central eclipses are concentric and only apparent size decides total vs annular. It shows the umbra length vs the distance to the ground. Lunar mode: Earth's umbra and penumbra at the Moon (Meeus's 1.02 enlargement). Checked against NASA's catalogue for every type (gamma ±0.01, size ratio ±0.001). Hybrid text uses the catalogue type. While the panel is open, `alignLine` (Sun → Earth, extended) is drawn in 3D.
- "Watch it from Earth" (`setSky` / `updateSky`): replaces the space render with `skyScene` / `skyCam` (the main loop renders one or the other). The Sun and Moon are drawn in their true directions at their true apparent sizes (display distances `SKY_SUN` / `SKY_MOON`). For a solar eclipse, the observer (`skyObs`) is fixed in Earth's spin frame at the point where the axis meets Earth at greatest eclipse, so playing time (set to 1 min/s) shows the Moon cross the Sun. Positions match NASA's greatest-eclipse locations to within about 0.5°. The dashed guide rings are the Moon's apparent size at perigee and apogee. For a lunar eclipse the view is from Earth's centre toward the anti-Sun point: the rings are the umbra and penumbra, and the shadow is shaded onto the Moon via `onBeforeCompile`. The sky Moon material is `transparent` so it sorts after the glow and corona sprites (three.js draws opaque before transparent regardless of `renderOrder`). Sky colour, glow and corona follow the obscured fraction of the Sun's area (`overlapArea`). The body panel, labels and hint are hidden via `body.sky`.
- What if… (fiction mode, `setFiction`): an N-body simulation replaces the formulas. `update()` calls `fictPlace()` instead of the ephemeris, and the frame loop calls `fictAdvance()`. The state lives in `F` (Float64Array `x`/`v`/`a` in AU and AU/day, barycentric ecliptic; `gm` in AU³/day² from `GM_KM`, planets without their moons). It's seeded at switch-on from `fictSnapshot()`, with Earth and Moon split out of JPL's EMB, moons from `moonKm()`, and velocities by central differences. With no edits it tracks the ephemeris to ~0.2° over 10 years. The integrator: heavy bodies (Sun, planets, Moon, Charon, the visiting star) use kick-drift-kick with a power-of-two step (0.05 × the tightest dynamical time, `F.tdyn`). Light moons (<0.5% of their host, `fictClassify`) are substepped relative to their host, under a differential perturbation held fixed for the step. A light moon pulled hard by something else is promoted to heavy. There's a ~30 ms work budget per frame (`F.lag`). Collisions merge (momentum conserved). `fictHosts()` finds what each body is bound to (bound, and inside that host's Hill sphere), which drives drawing positions (`hostDisplay`), lost/captured events and the impact report (compared with `F.base`, the real osculating orbits at switch-on). Trails are recorded inside steps (`fictSample`). The hollow `ghosts` show the real planet positions. Eclipses, Galaxy and Now are disabled while it's on. Fluid Roche limits (`fictRoche`, 2.44 R (ρ_big/ρ_small)^⅓) are reported as events, not modelled (bodies stay whole). Pairs already inside one at switch-on (Phobos) are pre-marked in `F.rocheSeen`. Presets can set a camera focus (4th entry), e.g. "Stop the Moon" (speed ×1e-4) follows Earth.
- What if… impacts (`IMP`, "impacts" section): the integrator flags collision courses (`fictCourse`: close pair whose two-body periapsis is below the sum of radii while closing, checked for heavy pairs in `fictAccel` and light moons in `fictStep`). `fictAdvance` stops the frame there, `impactSim` saves a snapshot (`fictSave`, for Replay) and flies the camera in (`impactFocus` via `impactCam` in `focusCamPos`). `impactCap` caps sim days per frame so the approach takes ~2.5 s, then holds and eases back. While approaching, `impactApproach` redraws the impactor so its display gap shrinks with the true gap (the display sizes are exaggerated). Inside the Roche limit it's drawn as a string of pieces that hit in turn. `fictMerge` calls `impactRecord` before merging. The effects (`impactShow`: flash, fireball, shockwave ring, ballistic debris) are illustrations in real seconds, scaled by `sev` from the energy. Scars (`addScar`: crater, rays, molten glow, gas-giant spots, global magma glow) are shells on the body's spin group that fade in sim days. Shell materials are MeshBasic patched in `onBeforeCompile`, because a plain ShaderMaterial loses the logarithmic depth buffer. `#impCard` shows speed, angle, energy (Mt, vs Hiroshima/Chicxulub), crater size (Collins, Melosh & Marcus 2005), and what it would mean. It opens after a short delay (longer on phones, where it's a sheet in `SHEETS`), and the "Details" link in the event list reopens it.
- Moon orbit ring is tilted 5.145° and turned to the ascending node `moonNode(d)` (= L − F of `moonGeo`, so ring and Moon agree). Solid half = north of ecliptic.
- "Why not every month?" (in the Eclipses panel): live readouts plus a geometry overlay (`geoGrp`: ecliptic disc, node line, Sun/shadow line). In that view `sceneRadius` shrinks Earth and Moon to true size relative to the Moon's orbit (60.27 Earth radii), because neither display mode keeps that ratio (true mode is only ~5 Earth radii) and the exaggerated sizes make the Moon look aligned every month. The camera turns with the Sun line to stay side-on.

## Phones and tablets
- Everything mobile is keyed to two media queries, so a desktop with a mouse is unchanged: **PHONE** = `(max-width: 760px), (pointer: coarse) and (max-height: 500px)` (landscape phones count), and **TOUCH** = `(pointer: coarse)`. Touch tablets (coarse, wider than 760 px) get a third CSS block.
- Phones: the bar keeps − / speed / + / Pause / Now / More. `layoutBar()` moves the other buttons (same elements, listeners intact) into `#morePop` and back when the query stops matching. `--ctrl-h` is the bar's measured height (ResizeObserver), and phone and tablet sheets sit above it. Safe areas use `env(safe-area-inset-*)`, and the page uses `100dvh`.
- One sheet at a time on PHONE or TOUCH: a MutationObserver on the panel, Eclipses, Galaxy, What if…, Orbits and More closes the others with their own close functions. For Galaxy and What if… that only hides the card (`toggleGalCard` / `toggleFictPop`); the mode stays on. Both cards have a Hide button there, and `#galChip` / `#fictChip` (top right) bring them back. On phones, picking a What if… scenario hides its panel so the result can be seen.
- Touch input: tap tolerance 12 px (mouse 5). A missed tap picks the nearest body within 28 px (`nearestBody`). In What if…, picking up a planet on touch needs a 350 ms long press (`fictGrab`); a mouse grabs at once.
- Touch devices load 1024 px maps from `textures/js/sm/` (`SMALL_MAPS`, falling back to the full map). They also use pixel ratio 1.5 and a 12 ms What if… budget (`FICT_BUDGET`). None of these change any physics.

## Display scaling
- `compressed` mode: distance = 12·√r(AU); body sizes exaggerated (∝ R^0.42).
- `true` mode: 1 AU = 40 units; planets ×400, Sun ×20; Moon distance exaggerated.

## Gotchas
- Each body object has 3D groups `root` / `tilt` / `spin`. Don't add data fields with those names (the axial-tilt number is `obliq` because `tilt` clashed once and crashed the app).

## Running locally
- Opening `kbosmos.html` from disk works in Chrome. The browser pane, though, renders `file://` pages as a snapshot without relative files, so test over HTTP. `.claude/launch.json` starts `.claude/serve.ps1`, a PowerShell static server on http://localhost:8765. Python and Node aren't installed on this machine.
